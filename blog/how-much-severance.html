1. Major Regional Minimum Benchmarks (2026)

🇺🇸 United States (USA)

There is no federal statutory entitlement to severance in the US. However, many states have mini-WARN Acts regulating notice periods for mass layoffs. Voluntary company guidelines universally apply a baseline **"1-week-per-year" formula** (1 week of base salary for every year of completed tenure), which increases to 2 weeks per year for senior managers and executives.

🇬🇧 United Kingdom (UK)

Statutory redundancy pay is mandated by UK labor code for workers with 2+ years of service:

  • Ages under 22: 0.5 week's pay per year.
  • Ages 22 to 40: 1 week's pay per year.
  • Ages 41 and over: 1.5 weeks' pay per year.
Weekly pay calculations are capped by standard government limits (currently updated to £700 in 2026).

🇨🇦 Canada

Canadian workers are protected by provincial minimum employment standards (often 1-2 weeks per year). Crucially, **Common Law reasonable notice** rules apply to most non-unionised terminations. Canadian courts frequently award between 2 to 4 weeks of full salary per year of service, with executive limits capped up to a maximum of 24 months.

🇦🇺 Australia

Australia’s National Employment Standards (NES) mandate a redundancy scale based on tenure:

  • 1-2 years: 4 weeks' pay.
  • 5-6 years: 10 weeks' pay.
  • 9-10 years: 16 weeks' pay.
Companies with fewer than 15 employees are exempt from statutory redundancy rules under small business exclusions.

🇵🇰 Pakistan

Workers in Pakistan are legally protected by the **Standing Orders Ordinance of 1968**. Employers must pay a statutory gratuity equivalent to **30 days' average wages** for every year of continuous service. This is one of the highest baseline statutory requirements worldwide.

2. Common Pitfalls When Estimating Severance

Many employees make avoidable errors when assessing their separation paperwork. Avoid these three common pitfalls:

  • Including Allowances: Most statutory formulas (particularly Middle Eastern end-of-service gratuities) are calculated on your **basic wage only**. Do not include housing, transport, or school subsidies unless your contract explicitly states they are part of the base calculation.
  • Forfeiting Accrued Vacation: In jurisdictions like California or New York, accrued unused vacation days are legally protected as wages and **must** be paid out upon separation. Ensure your employer doesn't sweep these away in your lump sum.
  • Accepting the First Offer: Employers expect a negotiation. Signing the "Release of Claims" immediately without review forfeits your leverage to request subsidies like health insurance extensions or outplacement resources.

3. High-Leverage Negotiation Checklist

When presenting your separation package counter-proposal, structure your requests clearly around these items:

Separation Negotiation Guide:

  1. Health Coverage: Ask the employer to pay for COBRA premiums or equivalent private health premiums for 3-6 months.
  2. Reference Clauses: Request a mutual "neutral or positive reference" clause in your agreement to protect your future career prospects.
  3. Bonus Pro-Rata: If you are separated mid-year, request your performance bonus to be paid out on a pro-rata basis.
  4. Non-Compete Release: If your agreement contains restrictive non-compete covenants, request a full or partial release from them in exchange for signing.

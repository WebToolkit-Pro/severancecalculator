Gratuity Rights in Pakistan

In Pakistan, gratuity is governed by the West Pakistan Industrial and Commercial Employment (standing Orders) Ordinance, 1968. It is a mandatory benefit for permanent employees in establishments with 20 or more workers.

The 30-Day Rule

Every permanent employee is entitled to a gratuity equivalent to 30 days' wages for every completed year of service (or any period over 6 months).

Authentic FAQ (Search Console Data)

Do I get gratuity if my company has a Provident Fund?
Under the standing Orders Ordinance, an employer is NOT required to pay gratuity if they have established a Provident Fund where the employer's contribution is at least equal to the gratuity amount.
Is gratuity payable on resignation?
Yes. Gratuity is payable upon termination of employment for any reason, including resignation, death, or retirement, provided you have completed the minimum service period (usually 1 year).

Provident Fund Exception

Under the Standing Orders Ordinance, an employer is exempt from paying statutory gratuity if they have established a recognized Provident Fund for the employee. However, this exception only applies if the employer's contribution to the Provident Fund is equal to or greater than the statutory gratuity amount (i.e., at least 8.33% of the basic salary). If the employer's contribution is less, they must pay the difference as gratuity.

Taxation on Gratuity in Pakistan

According to the Income Tax Ordinance 2001, the tax treatment of gratuity depends on whether the fund is approved by the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR):

  • Approved Gratuity Fund: The entire gratuity amount received is tax-exempt up to Rs. 300,000. Any amount exceeding this is subject to tax.
  • Approved Gratuity Scheme: Tax-exempt up to Rs. 200,000.
  • Unapproved Scheme: Exemption is limited to the lesser of Rs. 75,000 or 50% of the receivable amount.

Dispute Resolution & NIRC

If an employer fails to pay gratuity within the stipulated time, the aggrieved employee can file a grievance notice within 90 days of the cause of action. If unresolved, the employee may file a petition before the relevant Labor Court or the National Industrial Relations Commission (NIRC) under Section 33 of the Industrial Relations Act (IRA).

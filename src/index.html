The Ultimate Guide to Global Severance Pay (2026 Edition)

Everything you need to know about redundancy calculations, statutory limits, and labor law rights.

What is Severance Pay?

Severance pay (also known as redundancy pay, separation pay, or end-of-service gratuity) is a financial compensation package provided by an employer to an employee upon the termination of their employment contract. The primary purpose of this payout is to act as a financial bridge, helping the employee sustain their livelihood while they search for a new role. However, the legal obligation to pay severance—and the formula used to calculate it—varies dramatically depending on your geographic location, the length of your service, and the reason for your termination.

How Severance Works Across the Globe

Understanding global labor laws is critical for expatriates, remote workers, and HR professionals managing international teams. Let's break down how the formulas differ across major economic regions:

The Middle East (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar)

In the GCC (Gulf Cooperation Council) region, severance is legally defined as "End of Service Gratuity." Because expats typically do not have access to state pension funds, the gratuity acts as a mandatory retirement substitute. For example, under the UAE's 2026 Federal Decree-Law No. 33, an employee is entitled to 21 days of basic salary for each year of service for the first five years, and 30 days for each year thereafter. The critical caveat is that the calculation strictly uses the Basic Salary, stripping away housing, transport, and other allowances.

The United Kingdom & Europe

The UK employs a highly structured, age-bracketed system for Statutory Redundancy Pay. Under the Employment Rights Act, an employee receives 0.5 weeks' pay for each full year worked while under age 22, 1 week's pay for years worked between 22 and 41, and 1.5 weeks' pay for years worked aged 41 or older. Crucially, the UK government caps the weekly salary used in this calculation (updated annually) and limits the maximum tenure payout to 20 years. Other European nations, such as Germany and France, rely heavily on union-negotiated collective bargaining agreements alongside statutory minimums.

The United States

Unlike Europe and the Middle East, the United States operates primarily under "At-Will Employment" laws. The Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) does not mandate severance pay. However, corporate norms often dictate offering 1 to 2 weeks of severance for every year of service, especially during mass layoffs, in exchange for the employee signing a release of liability. The Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act provides some protections, requiring 60 days' notice for mass layoffs, but statutory severance formulas are rare outside of specific union contracts or executive agreements.

Asia-Pacific (India, Philippines, Australia)

In India, the Payment of Gratuity Act of 1972 mandates that any employee with 5 continuous years of service must receive a payout calculated as (15/26) × Basic Salary × Years of Service. The Philippines Labor Code mandates Separation Pay for authorized causes (like redundancy or disease), typically equating to one month's pay or at least one-half month's pay for every year of service, depending on the cause. Australia governs redundancy through the National Employment Standards (NES), tying the weeks of severance directly to tenure tiers.

The Importance of "Reason for Termination"

Your eligibility for a payout hinges entirely on why you are leaving the company. Generally, there are four scenarios:

  • Layoff / Redundancy: This is an employer-initiated termination without cause (e.g., role elimination, company downsizing). In almost all jurisdictions, this triggers the maximum statutory payout.
  • Resignation: If you quit voluntarily, many Western countries offer zero severance. However, in the Middle East and India, you are still entitled to your accrued gratuity, provided you meet the minimum vesting period (typically 1 to 5 years).
  • Dismissal For Cause (Fired): If you are terminated for gross misconduct (e.g., theft, fraud, severe policy violations), you forfeit your right to severance or gratuity globally.
  • Mutual Agreement: When both parties agree to part ways, the severance amount is entirely negotiable and is usually codified in a legally binding settlement agreement.

Tax Implications of Your Payout

Calculating your gross severance is only half the battle; understanding your net take-home pay is equally vital. Tax authorities treat severance differently. In the UK, the first £30,000 of redundancy pay is generally tax-free. In India, gratuity received under the Gratuity Act is exempt from income tax up to a specified limit (currently ₹20 Lakhs). Conversely, the IRS in the United States taxes severance pay as supplemental income, which may push you into a higher tax bracket for the year. Always consult a certified tax professional in your jurisdiction before finalizing a settlement.

Negotiating Your Severance Package

Statutory minimums are just that—minimums. Depending on your leverage, you can often negotiate a better package. When entering negotiations, consider requesting an extension of health insurance benefits (like COBRA in the US), accelerated vesting of stock options, or outplacement services. Use tools like our calculator above to determine your legal baseline, then build your negotiation strategy from there. Remember, employers primarily offer severance above the statutory minimum to secure your signature on a non-disparagement and liability waiver.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Severance pay and who is entitled to it?
Severance pay is compensation given to an employee upon termination of employment. Eligibility depends on the reason for termination and length of service.
How is UAE gratuity calculated in 2026?
Under the 2026 UAE Labour Law, gratuity is calculated based on 21 days of basic salary for each year of the first 5 years, and 30 days for each additional year.
Is Severance pay taxable?
Taxation varies by country. In the UK, the first £30,000 is usually tax-free. In the USA, it is generally treated as regular income.
